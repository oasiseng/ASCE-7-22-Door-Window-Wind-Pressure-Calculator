Estimates ultimate (LRFD) and ASD wind pressures on doors and windows — components and cladding, wall Zones 4 and 5 — for low-rise enclosed or partially enclosed buildings per ASCE 7-22 Chapter 30, Part 1.

How to use this calculator
  1. Wind speed: Enter the ultimate wind speed (V) in mph from the ASCE 7-22 maps (or the ASCE Hazard Tool) for your risk category and location.
  2. Building dimensions: Input the length, width, and mean roof height (h) in feet. The low-rise method applies for h ≤ 60 ft.
  3. Exposure category: Choose B (urban/suburban), C (open terrain), or D (coastal/flat) based on surrounding terrain.
  4. Enclosure: Enclosed or partially open buildings use GCpi = ±0.18; partially enclosed (e.g., large unprotected openings on one wall) use ±0.55 per Table 26.13-1.
  5. Topographic factor (Kzt): 1.0 for flat sites; compute per ASCE Fig. 26.8-1 for hills, ridges, or escarpments.
  6. Roof slope (θ): For θ ≤ 10°, wall GCp values are reduced 10% per the Fig. 30.3-1 footnote.
  7. Ground elevation: Optional. Site elevation above sea level sets Ke per Table 26.9-1 (leave 0 for the conservative Ke = 1.0).
  8. Effective wind area: The component's tributary area (sq ft). GCp is log-interpolated between A = 10 and A = 500 sq ft.

Zones: Zone 5 is the end zone within distance 'a' of building corners; Zone 4 is the remaining wall area. The diagram in the results shows where each applies.

Outputs: Ultimate pressures are strength-level (1.0W). ASD values are 0.6 × ultimate, the level usually compared against window/door DP ratings — confirm with your product approval and AHJ. A 16 psf strength-level minimum is enforced per Section 30.2.2.

Parameters

1.0 = flat site. Typical hill/escarpment values run 1.1–1.6.
Optional. 0 = sea level (Ke = 1.0, always conservative).
